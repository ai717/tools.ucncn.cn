📖 International Morse Code Guide: History, Timing & Audio Standards

Invented in the 1830s by Samuel Morse and Alfred Vail, Morse Code revolutionized global telecommunications by encoding text characters into standardized sequences of short and long signals, known phonetically as dits (dots .) and dahs (dashes -).

❓ What are the standard Morse code timing rules?

According to the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R M.1677-1) standard:
• The length of a dot (dit) is 1 unit of time.
• A dash (dah) equals 3 units of time.
• The space between dots and dashes within the same letter is 1 unit.
• The space between distinct letters is 3 units.
• The space between words is 7 units (represented by / in text).

❓ Why is SOS the universal distress signal?

In 1905, the German radio regulations adopted ... --- ... (SOS) because its distinct pattern—three dots, three dashes, three dots—is immediately recognizable and virtually impossible to misinterpret through heavy atmospheric static, unlike earlier distress codes like CQD.

❓ How do I enter spaces between words in Morse code?

Separate individual Morse letter characters with a single space. Separate complete words with a forward slash / or multiple spaces (e.g., .... . .-.. .-.. --- / .-- --- .-. .-.. -.. for "HELLO WORLD").
